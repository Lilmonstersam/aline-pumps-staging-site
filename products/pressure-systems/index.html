    Where they are used

    Common Applications

    Pressure systems have versatile applications, providing water pressure and flow in various settings. They ensure consistent water supply in residential and commercial environments, including high-rise buildings, hospitals, and universities. In agricultural and horticultural applications, they support irrigation and greenhouse operations. These systems play a crucial role in rainwater harvesting and stormwater reuse, aiding in water recycling. They are also used in infrastructure projects and industrial environments like warehouses and processing plants, ensuring steady water pressure for diverse needs.

    FAQ

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the advantages of having a pressure booster pump?
    A pressure booster pump ensures reliable water pressure and flow, which is critical for fire protection systems, plumbing installations, and large commercial facilities. It helps maintain consistent performance, prevents downtime, and supports compliance with system requirements in demanding applications.
    What is the difference between a transfer pump and a pressure pump?
    A transfer pump is designed to move water from one location to another without necessarily increasing pressure. It’s commonly used in drainage or water transfer applications. A pressure pump, however, is built to increase the pressure of water being delivered to outlets, ensuring consistent and powerful flow.
    Where should a water pressure booster be installed?
    A pressure booster pump is typically installed near the main water inlet, after the water meter and before distribution to the building. In multi-storey buildings, it may be placed on the ground floor or in a mechanical plant room for optimal coverage. Always follow manufacturer and plumbing guidelines for safe installation.
